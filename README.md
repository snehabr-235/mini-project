# Power BI Mini Project – Employment & AI Risk Analysis

## 1. Project Overview

This project is an interactive **Power BI dashboard** designed to analyze employment trends, workforce distribution, industry performance, and potential **AI-related employment risks**.

The dashboard provides a visual and interactive way to understand employee distribution based on:

* Industry
* Employment Status
* Education
* Location
* Date/Year
* AI Risk
* Age Group

The project contains multiple dashboard pages that allow users to explore employment information and identify workforce groups that may be affected by AI and automation.

---

## 2. Objectives

The main objectives of this project are:

1. To analyze the overall employment situation across different industries.
2. To understand the distribution of employees by education level.
3. To analyze employed and unemployed workforce groups.
4. To visualize workforce distribution geographically.
5. To study employment trends over time.
6. To identify employees with different levels of AI-related employment risk.
7. To analyze AI risk across industries and education groups.
8. To provide meaningful insights and recommendations for workforce planning.

---

## 3. Tools and Technologies Used

* **Microsoft Power BI**

# 7. Page 3 – Employment Insight Analysis

The final page presents the major findings from the dashboard in an easy-to-understand format.

The insights include:

### Employment

The selected Finance segment contains **248 employees**, including:

* **200 employed employees**
* **48 unemployed employees**
* Employment rate: approximately **80.65%**
* Unemployment rate: approximately **19.35%**

This indicates that the selected Finance segment has a predominantly employed workforce.

### Education

Within the selected Finance segment, the largest education groups include:

* PhD – 58 employees
* Master's – 49 employees
* Bachelor's – 47 employees

This indicates a workforce with a relatively strong representation of higher education qualifications.

### Employment Trend

Employment levels vary over time, with the dashboard showing a higher level around **2027**, followed by a decline in **2028**.

This trend should be interpreted according to the available records in the dataset.

### AI Risk

AI-related employment risk is distributed across several education groups.

Bachelor's degree holders represent the largest share of AI-risk employees, followed by Master's and PhD holders.

This indicates that AI-related employment risk is **not limited to employees with lower educational qualifications**.

# 8. Key Insights

The dashboard provides the following major insights:

1. Employment distribution varies considerably across different industries.

2. The Finance segment in the selected view contains **248 employees**, with the majority being employed.

3. Higher education groups such as Bachelor's, Master's and PhD holders represent significant portions of the workforce.

4. AI-related employment risk exists across multiple education levels.

5. AI risk can affect employees who are currently employed, meaning AI-related risk is not limited to unemployment.

6. Industries such as **Retail, Technology and Healthcare** require attention when considering AI-related workforce changes.

7. Employment and AI-risk trends should be monitored over time to identify groups that may require reskilling.

# 9. Recommendations

Based on the analysis, the following recommendations can be made:

* Provide **AI and digital-skills training** to employees working in industries with higher AI-risk levels.
* Develop **reskilling and upskilling programs** for employees whose jobs may be affected by automation.
* Encourage continuous learning across different education groups.
* Monitor AI-risk trends over time.
* Identify industries with increasing AI exposure and prepare employees for changing job requirements.
* Use AI-risk analysis to support workforce planning and future training investments.
